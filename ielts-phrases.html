1. OPINION & POSITION STATEMENTS
Use these to clearly position yourself at the start of any Part 3 answer.
1I would argue that…stronger than "I think" — sounds academic
2I'd say it really hinges on…"зависит от" — sophisticated verb
3As I see it, the crux of the matter is…"суть вопроса в том, что"
4I'm inclined to believe that…"я склонен полагать, что"
5From my perspective, the most compelling argument is…"самый убедительный аргумент"
6If I had to weigh in, I'd say…"если бы я высказался…"
7It strikes me that…"мне бросается в глаза, что"
8I take a somewhat different view on this…polite way to disagree with examiner
Example: "I'd say it really hinges on the context. In some professions, working from home is perfectly viable, whereas in others, it would be utterly impractical."
2. LINKING & COHERENCE DEVICES
These connect your ideas naturally and show sophisticated discourse.
1What I'm getting at is…"то, к чему я веду, это"
2To put it another way,…rephrase previous point
3That said, / Having said that,…transition to contrast
4What is more,…add a stronger point
5The thing is,…explain the real issue
6By and large,…"в целом"
7In a broader sense,…move to abstract level
8More importantly,…highlight key reason
9Not only…but also…add emphasis to two things
10Bear in mind that…introduce a crucial point
3. HEDGING & ACADEMIC TONE
Show you can speak with nuance — never too absolute.
1It tends to be the case that…general pattern without overstating
2There's a general consensus that…everyone roughly agrees
3It's widely assumed that…common belief
4To a certain extent,…partially true
5In some respects, this holds true,…"в некоторых отношениях"
6One could argue that…impersonal / academic
7It would be fair to say that…balanced judgement
8I'm by no means an expert, but…humble tone, very natural
4. PART 2 — STORYTELLING PHRASES
Use to extend your 2-minute story naturally — examiners love these.
1It all started when…opening a story
2Looking back, what stands out is…retrospective emphasis
3What I remember most vividly is…highlight a key memory
4It turned out that…surprise / unexpected twist
5Little did I know that…foreshadow something
6As it happened,…introduce a coincidence
7I ended up doing something I never imagined I'd…surprise / unexpected outcome
8Ironically enough,…contrast in expectation
9Looking back on it now,…reflective closing
10It's funny how…natural reflection
Example (cue card: "Describe a memorable event"):
"It all started when my sister called me on a random Tuesday afternoon, asking if I'd be free that weekend. Looking back, what stands out is how spontaneous the whole thing was — we hadn't planned anything in advance, yet it turned out to be one of those days I'll be telling my grandchildren about."
5. PART 2 — DESCRIBING & EMPHASISING
Replace "very" and "really" with these for instant band 8.
1absolutely fascinatinginstead of "very interesting"
2incredibly rewardingfor experiences, hobbies
3utterly exhaustingstrong emphasis on tiredness
4remarkably well-preservedfor buildings, objects
5genuinely memorableinstead of "very memorable"
6a tad overrated"немного переоценён" — sophisticated
7nowhere near as good as…compare down
8twice as challenging as…comparative emphasis
9a far cry from what I expectedexpectation vs reality
10the very thought of it makes me…strong emotional reaction
Never use "very" + adjective. Use: remarkably, incredibly, absolutely, utterly, genuinely, thoroughly.
6. PART 3 — DISCUSSION OPENERS
Never start with "Yes" or "I think". Open with these instead.
1It's a complex issue, but if I had to single out one thing,…framing device
2There's no straightforward answer, but…intellectual honesty
3I suppose the real question is whether…re-frame the question
4If anything, I'd argue that the opposite is true.strong counter-statement
5That's a thought-provoking question.buy time + show engagement
6It really boils down to whether…"по сути, вопрос в том"
7I can see both sides of the argument, but…balanced opening
8On the face of it,…"на первый взгляд"
7. PART 3 — GIVING REASONS & EXAMPLES
Always justify your opinion. Use these instead of "because" 10 times.
1The main reason behind this is that…first reason
2This is largely attributable to the fact that…formal "because of"
3One factor that comes into play here is…introduce a cause
4A case in point would be…strong academic example
5To illustrate this point,…introduce example
6By way of example, in my own country…personal/cultural example
7This is particularly evident in…specific area
8What this boils down to is…simplify the argument
Example: "Why do young people move abroad?" → "This is largely attributable to the fact that employment opportunities in their home countries are often limited, particularly in specialised fields. A case in point would be the brain drain that many Eastern European countries have experienced over the past two decades."
8. PART 3 — CONTRAST & CONCESSION
Show you can discuss both sides — examiners reward this heavily.
1I take your point, but I'd counter that…respectful disagreement
2While it's true that…, I'd still argue that…concession + position
3Granted, …, but on balance,…acknowledge + conclude
4That may well be the case, and yet…sophisticated contrast
5Conversely,…"с другой стороны" — high-band
6Be that as it may,…formal "тем не менее"
7The flip side of the coin is that…"обратная сторона"
8On a more practical note,…shift from theory to practice
9. PART 3 — CONCLUDING & SUMMARISING
Wrap up your Part 3 answers like a pro.
1All things considered,…"учитывая всё"
2On balance, I'd say that…weighing both sides
3In the long run, I suppose…long-term perspective
4Ultimately, it comes down to…final reduction
5Time will tell whether…future-oriented close
6Whether or not that's a good thing depends on…hedged conclusion
7At the end of the day,…natural conversational close
10. CONDITIONALS (GRAMMAR FOR BAND 8)
Mix these into Part 3 — they instantly upgrade your grammatical range.
Mixed Conditional — Present result, past cause
If I had studied harder at school, I would have more options now.
If she had moved abroad ten years ago, she would be much more established today.
Inverted Conditional (advanced)
Were the government to invest more in education, this trend could easily be reversed.
Had I been in that position, I would have made a different choice.
Unless / Provided that / As long as
Provided that students are taught critical thinking from an early age, they tend to perform better academically.
As long as the policy is implemented consistently, it should yield positive results.
Hypothetical past
If I were to design a new city, I would prioritise green spaces over highways.
11. INVERSION & CLEFT SENTENCES
Use sparingly but impactfully — these are pure band 8-9 territory.
Negative inversion
Not only does technology save us time, but it also reshapes how we think.
Never before have so many people had access to higher education.
Hardly anyone disputes the importance of basic literacy.
Under no circumstances should children be exposed to such content.
Cleft sentences (emphasis)
What I find most concerning is the speed at which this is happening.
It was the lack of public transport that eventually drove people to leave.
What really matters is whether the policy is enforceable.
12. PERFECT & CONTINUOUS TENSES
Mix past simple with these — examiners look for tense variety.
Present Perfect Continuous — ongoing action with present relevance
I've been reading more and more non-fiction lately, and it's changed the way I see the world.
Researchers have been debating this issue for decades.
Past Perfect — earlier past event
By the time I realised what was happening, the opportunity had already passed.
She had never travelled abroad before she got that scholarship.
Future Perfect — looking forward
By 2030, AI will have transformed most white-collar jobs.

1hit the nail on the head"попасть в точку"
2the tip of the iceberg"верхушка айсберга"
3go the extra mileсделать больше, чем требуется
4miss the point entirelyсовсем не уловить суть
5a blessing in disguise"не было бы счастья, да несчастье помогло"
6cost an arm and a leg"стоит бешеных денег"
7it's easier said than done"легко сказать, трудно сделать"
8add fuel to the fire"подлить масла в огонь"
9a stepping stone to something bigger"ступенька к чему-то большему"
10on the same wavelength"на одной волне"
Idioms work best in Part 2 (stories) and as occasional emphasis in Part 3. Don't force 5 idioms into one answer.
15. SMART FILLER PHRASES
Replace "uhhh" / "you know" with these — shows fluency.
1Well, where do I even begin…when you don't know where to start
2How shall I put it…buying time to think
3Let me think about that for a second…natural pause
4Actually, no — let me rephrase that.show self-correction skill
5That's a really good question, actually.buy 3 seconds of time
6I suppose what I'm trying to say is…recovering after hesitating
7Off the top of my head,…spontaneous opinion
8I never thought about it that way before, but…honest reflection
These are MUCH better than "um" / "uh" / "er". They signal that you're thinking, not struggling.
